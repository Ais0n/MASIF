#  Specialized Working Groups 
  1. 
  2. 
  3. Specialized Working Groups
EHA’s Specialized Working Groups (SWGs) foster science and spread knowledge in basic, translational, and clinical research in hematology.
To ensure we cover the full field of hematology and provide a research platform for all groups and researchers, we’ve established two types of SWG:
  * **Disease-oriented SWGs** —which cover specific topics such as leukemias and lymphomas
  * **Overarching SWGs** —which cover broad themes such as aging and precision medicine
This model is based on the nine sections of the .
There are currently 25 SWGs. You can use the filter below to view them by type.
## How to join an SWG
If you’re interested in making new connections or sharing your expertise, you can apply to join an SWG. To do this, just follow the instructions on the main page of the relevant SWG.
## SWG Grants
EHA provides financial support to small research projects initiated by our SWGs. You can find out more about his support, and discover some of the projects we’ve already supported, in the .

## Overview
The EHA Lymphoma Group (EHA LyG) was set up with a mission to advance the understanding, diagnostics, and targeted treatment of lymphoma. We achieve this by fostering closer collaboration between various lymphoma groups, leading to enhanced knowledge exchange and education. 
We are a pan-European network of national groups, scientists, and hematologists covering all major areas of lymphoma research, including: 
  * Hodgkin’s lymphoma 
  * Indolent and aggressive non-Hodgkin’s lymphoma 
  * Waldenström’s macroglobulinemia 
Our priority areas are: 
  * Improving the understanding of the pathogenesis/biology of lymphoma
  * Improving the diagnostics in patients with lymphoma (including new imaging techniques and molecular characterization of lymphoma) 
  * Improving the (targeted) treatment of lymphoma
  * Fostering closer collaboration between lymphoma groups 
  * Optimizing the exchange and dissemination of knowledge and information, including improvement in education 
We work in close partnership with the European Mantle Cell Network and ELI, the European Lymphoma Institute. Our goal is to become an organization that facilitates investigator-initiated, cross-border clinical trials and registry studies. We aim to do this across different lymphoma subtypes, with a focus on rare types of lymphoma.

## Overview
The Adult Acute Lymphoblastic Leukemia (Adult ALL) SWG is dedicated to furthering our understanding and spreading knowledge of the treatment of this condition. 
We’re a community of members that consists mostly of the European Working Group for Adult ALL (EWALL) group. The Adult ALL SWG includes the leaders of each European national ALL study groups.
Our areas of focus are:
  * Diagnostics—the identification of new genetic markers, particularly to define the new ALL subgroups ETP (Early T-Precursor ALL), bcr-abl-like-ALL
  * Minimal residual disease—the EWALL study group is leading in the standardization of the different methods for MRD, particularly immunophenotyping, RT-PCR, and ongoing, NGS
  * The treatment and patient care of adolescents and young adults (AYA)
  * The treatment and patient care in older ALL patients
  * The development of joint treatment approaches including prospective clinical trials
  * The exploration of targeted therapies—EWALL is preparing in sequential studies of 1st, 2nd, and 3rd generation tyrosine kinase inhibitors
  * The development of targeted therapies by immunotherapy
  * The development of joint meta-analyses
  * Ensuring the cooperation of the pharmaceutical industry for the optimal evaluation of new compounds in ALL, either in company sponsored or investigator-initiated trials  
  * Develop guidelines and consensus recommendations for the management of ALL

## Overview
The objective of the SWG is the cure of Chronic Myeloid Leukemia (CML) by cooperative research. 
The CML SWG: 
  * Sits centrally within a range of national and international clinical, translational, and basic science activities in CML
  * Uses its expertise to identify novel findings of importance to the wider community
Through the annual EHA Congress and outreach meetings, we aim to educate, update, and synthesize new information to reach the widest possible audience.
